**Verification convention** (the guide asks us to distinguish measured / source / hypothesis):

- **[S]** Reliable secondary source (agricultural press: Réussir, Terre-net, Pleinchamp) citing an official source, or a search snippet from an official source that could not be opened (Terres Inovia and HAL block automated access with a 403). Must be checked before it goes on a slide.

## 0. Most important for the pitch (TL;DR)

1. **The guide's "4 %" is not "pulses for eating".** The 4 % (about 1 Mha) in the 2020 plan adds up soybean, pea (pois), dry pulses, **alfalfa and forage legumes**, that is, mostly forage. Grain legumes are about 430.000 ha (2024), or about 1,6 % of the SAU (*surface agricole utile*, utilised agricultural area), and direct human-food pulses (lentil, chickpea, dry bean (poroto)) are a small fraction of that. A technical jury may ask about this, so we should say it first. [V]/[S]
2. **The 2030 target changed number:** 8 % of the SAU (2 Mha) in the 2020 strategy, 10 % of the SAU in the minister's speech in July 2025. Cite both with their dates. [V]
3. **The areas are not taking off:** pea 150.000 ha (2023) → 124.000 (2024) → 98.000 (2025); Occitanie went from 100.000 ha of pulses (2020) to 80.000 (2024). [S]
4. **France produces about 1/3 of the plant proteins it consumes in human food** (ministry, 2020). In lentil the deficit is clear: 43.000 t imported in 2024/25 against 45.000 t produced in 2025. [V]/[S]
5. **The bruchid (*bruche*) is the hard line between "food" and "feed":** at most 3 % bruchid-damaged grain for human food and 10 % for animal feed (France); Egypt, the first buyer of French faba bean for human consumption, requires 1 %. There is no OAD (*outil d'aide à la décision*, decision support tool) for the bruchid. [V]/[S]
6. **In the 2025 edition of Hack the Fork there was no agricultural-side project.** The podium was Ardoise (EGalim school canteens, the French food law), ReaKt (2nd, fermentation digital twin) and BlendIn (3rd, recipe reformulation). The producer side of Track 3 is open ground. [V]

### 1.2 Import dependence (human food)

- **"En ce qui concerne l'alimentation humaine, l'agriculture française produit environ un tiers de la consommation nationale, alors même que celle-ci est en forte croissance"** [As far as human food is concerned, French agriculture produces about a third of national consumption, even though that consumption is growing strongly] (plant proteins). [V] 2020 press kit: https://agriculture.gouv.fr/telecharger/122401. The same document says that for animal feed France produces half of the protein-rich materials (3/4 if grazing and forage are counted).
- Dry pulse consumption: 1,7 kg per person per year in France against a European average of 3,9 kg, divided by 4 in 20 years. [V] Same document (2020 data; already dated).
- 2024/25 imports (Observatoire OléoProtéines via Terre-net): **lentil 43.000 t (+13 %)**, chickpea 11.000 t (-17 %), faba bean 35.000 t, pea 40.000 t, soybean 777.000 t. France is in deficit for soybean and lentil, and in surplus for pea, chickpea and faba bean. [S] https://www.terre-net.fr/debouches/article/889517/les-legumineuses-confirment-leur-ancrage-dans-les-assiettes-francaises
- 2025 production (Terres Univia via Réussir): lentil 45.000 t (≈15 q/ha), chickpea 40.000 t (≈16,5 q/ha), pea 345.000 t (35,6 q/ha), faba bean 309.000 t (26,7 q/ha). "La qualité a été dégradée par les bioagresseurs en lentille mais s'est révélée très bonne en pois chiche." [Quality was degraded by pests in lentil but turned out to be very good in chickpea.] [S] https://www.reussir.fr/production-doleoproteagineux-2025-la-france-reste-le-premier-producteur-de-lunion-europeenne (the URL returned a 404 when opened; the text comes from the search index).
- **Own calculation [NV]:** imported lentil ≈ national production, so lentil self-sufficiency is around 50 % (not counting exports or stocks). Implied area: about 30.000 ha of lentil and 24.000 ha of chickpea (production / yield).
- Extreme variability: in 2021 lentil yields were divided by 5 (Anils) and production covered about 20 % of French demand (FNLS). [S] https://www.terre-net.fr/observatoire-technique-culturale/strategie-technique-culturale/article/une-annee-de-moissons-desastreuses-pour-toutes-les-lentilles-de-france-217-202371.html
- Watch out: FranceAgriMer's sovereignty indicators for "pois" are inconsistent between versions (137 % in one, 9 % in another). **Do not use them.** https://www.franceagrimer.fr/sites/default/files/2026-03/DIA-MUL-Souverainete_alimentaire-MAJ_2022-2024.pdf

## 2. Existing tools and competitors by idea

### Idea 3: bruchid damage risk ranking (lentil/faba bean) for cooperatives

- **Terres Inovia** (the French technical institute for oilseeds and protein crops) has around 15 online DSS (Myvar for varietal choice in pea, faba bean and lentil; Acacia for cover crops; a flea beetle DSS for rapeseed), **but none for the bruchid**. [S] https://www.terresinovia.fr/en/tools/myvar
- **BSV (plant health bulletins) for pulses:** they issue weekly alerts with weather rules (Tmax ≥20 °C for 2 consecutive days with young pods of 2 cm). It is a qualitative rule, not a per-plot damage model. [V] Terres Inovia guide; example BSV: https://draaf.centre-val-de-loire.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/bsv_proteagineux_n15-17062025.pdf
- **De Sangosse FabaOdor** (kairomone trap): the manufacturer declares -50 % bruchid-damaged grain. It is biocontrol, not prediction, and therefore **complementary**: our ranking could say where to place the traps. [S] https://www.reussir.fr/grandes-cultures/feverole-des-leurres-olfactifs-pour-detourner-les-bruches-de-la-culture
- **INRAE / AgroParisTech research (Gardarin et al.):** the dataset of 105 plots (2019-2022, 4 regions, Etalab 2.0 licence), DOI 10.57745/HPEMYW. [V] Metadata: https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/54ff84f0-37c5-595b-9248-4a4bb4bc6cf7. What they found:
  - The paper in *Pest Management Science* 2026 (https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-05526495v1) shows that damage is homogeneous within the plot, which indicates strong dispersion. [S]
  - The IOBC summary says that bruchid density follows **the area of the host crop the previous year**, that woodland increases it in lentil, that hedgerows reduce it in faba bean, and that plot-level practices have no detectable effect, so they call for landscape-scale strategies. [S] https://iobc-wprs.org/product/managing-populations-of-bruchid-pests-in-grain-legumes-role-of-resource-provisioning-at-field-and-landscape-levels/
  - **Implication:** the authors have already done the explanatory analysis. Our contribution has to be **predictive and operational** (an out-of-sample ranking to decide which lots to inspect, separate or treat in storage), not "discovering the drivers".
- **Sencrop / Weenat:** I found no bruchid models. They do have disease models for other crops. [NV] on whether something unpublished exists.
- **Optical sorting / post-harvest:** Trescartes and Pissier (2025 winners) invest in post-harvest treatment of lentil. They are natural clients for a "pre-sorting" of lots. [V] https://agriculture.gouv.fr/telecharger/150164

### Idea 5: multispectral drone yield estimation (pea/chickpea, US data)

- **Already published with these same data:** Zhang, McGee, Vandemark & Sankaran (2021), *Frontiers in Plant Science*, doi:10.3389/fpls.2021.640259. Multispectral UAV, pea and chickpea trials in Pullman/Fairfield WA and Genesee ID, 2017-2019, LASSO, with **test R² of 0,91 (chickpea) and 0,80 (pea)**. [V] https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2021.640259/full. The corresponding dataset is Umani et al. 2023 (*Data in Brief*). [S]
- Liu et al. 2024 (*Drones*): pea yield with RGB + MS fusion, R² up to 0,81. [S] https://doaj.org/article/b231f81fe219492b8edc48be4ed8d084
- Commercial: **Hiphen** (Avignon) does drone phenotyping for breeders; Farmstar (Airbus/Arvalis) is satellite-based and oriented to wheat, barley and rapeseed; xarvio Field Manager does not mention pulses. [S] https://www.seedworld.com/us/2023/11/30/there-is-a-better-way-drone-advances-drive-crop-breeding-innovation/ ; https://www.gpsworld.com/airbus-farmstar-service-improves-with-new-recommendations/ ; https://www.precisionag.com/regions/europe/bayer-digital-farming-product-now-available-for-european-farmers/
- **Rigor risk:** if we show an R² below 0,8 with the same data, the jury (or an INRAE mentor) may point out that the published baseline is better.

### Idea 4: early departmental yield forecast (climate + Agreste)

- **Agreste *conjoncture*** (short-term statistics): national monthly estimates of area, yield and production of pea and faba bean (for example at 1/7/2026: pea 31,0 q/ha, faba bean 24,6 q/ha). [S] https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/moisson-2026-rendements-en-baisse-jachere-en-hausse. The DRAAFs (regional directorates of agriculture and forestry) publish regional bulletins (for example https://draaf.centre-val-de-loire.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/no8_bimestrielle_juin_2026.pdf).
- **JRC MARS Bulletin** (monthly crop monitoring by the EU Joint Research Centre): quantitative monthly forecast **at country level**, which includes "protein crops (peas, field beans)". The sub-national level (NUTS3) is under research, not published. [V] https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/scientific-activities/crop-monitoring-and-yield-forecasting_en
- **EarthDaily/Geosys, Kayrros:** commercial satellite forecasts, mostly for cereals and oilseeds; I found no pulses at departmental level. [S] https://earthdaily.com/blog/earthdaily-awarded-a-480000-french-government-grant-to-support-agricultural-intelligence-for-ukraine
- **Gap:** lentil and chickpea at departmental level, with uncertainty and before May-June. We must confirm that Agreste has departmental series for lentil and chickpea (SAA sometimes aggregates them under "légumes secs"). [NV]
- **Data discrepancy to keep in mind:** pea 2025 gives 98.000 ha (Terres Univia), but Agreste 2026 gives 150.000 ha "purs ou mélangés" [pure or mixed]. The scope differs, and if the model uses Agreste this must be explained.

### Idea 2: climate analog seasons for a department

- **Climadiag Agriculture** (Météo-France + Solagro, successor to CANARI, July 2024): free, around 200-250 agroclimatic indicators, 8 km grid, 2030/2050/2100 horizons. It is a **future projection**, not a search for past analog seasons, and I found no specific indicators for pulses. [S] https://www.reussir.fr/climadiag-agriculture-le-service-gratuit-de-meteo-france-et-solagro-pour-sadapter-au-rechauffement ; https://solagro.org/images/imagesCK/files/focus/2024_Webinaire_Climadiag_Agriculture.pdf
- Climate analogs are a classic method (meteorology, CCAFS). The new element would be applying it to "this season resembles 2016/2021, so what happened to lentil in this department?". [NV] I found no French commercial product that does that.

### What the Hack the Fork jurors rewarded (Paris 2025 edition, 13-14/12/2025, École 42, 21 teams)

Source [V]: official report https://hackthefork.org/reports/HtF%202025%20-%20report.pdf

- **1st, Ardoise:** EGalim compliance in school canteens. Public data + generative AI for menus, cost and environment. A very clear user (the canteen manager) and a legal obligation as the driver.
- **2nd, ReaKt:** precision fermentation digital twin. Deep learning, error <2 % in biomass peaks, batch failures going from 8 % to ~0 % (simulated). *Note: the brief says "runner-up", and that is correct: ReaKt came 2nd. A press article calls it the "winning team"; the official report says 2nd.*
- **3rd, BlendIn:** recipe reformulation for cost, CO₂ and nutrition (-50 % CO₂, -30 % cost in pilot recipes).
- Mentions: EggForgeAI and AlgaeLoc (certified data for algae).
- The 2025 jury included BPI (Sector Lead Agri & Food), SIAL, Ecotrophelia, Land&Monkeys, ex France Digitale, an economist and Blackbox AI. **There were no agronomists.** Agronomy has to be framed in business language.
- **Pattern:** a concrete user + a quantified legal obligation or economic pain + an impact number (even if simulated, declared as such). **There was no agricultural or pulse project in 2025** (I reviewed the full report).

**Value of avoiding a downgrade (illustrative calculation, [NV], to be marked as a hypothesis in the pitch):**

- **Faba bean:** a lot that moves from the human channel to feed loses 30-40 €/t. At 26,7 q/ha (2025) that is **about 80-110 €/ha**. If aimed at Egypt (1 % threshold), the premium may be higher, but that data is old.
- **Lentil:** if a lot with more than 3 % damage is downgraded or needs costly optical sorting, and assuming a feed value of 200-250 €/t against 500-600 €/t for human food, 250-400 €/t is lost. At 15 q/ha that is **about 375-600 €/ha**, all hypothesis.
- **Plausible model:** a B2B licence charged to the cooperative per hectare of pulses collected (on the order of 5-10 €/ha, anchored on Farmstar), with an explainable ROI if it avoids downgrading a small percentage of lots. It must be validated with a cooperative mentor during the weekend.

## 4. Evaluation by idea

Scale 1-10. "Fit" = fit with Track 3 (production for human food + producer income) and with the impact criterion (30 %).

### Idea 3: bruchid damage risk in lentil/faba bean, for cooperatives

- **Innovation: 7/10.** There is no bruchid DSS (BSV 2019, and I found none for 2024-2026). The open dataset is from 2026 and the authors did explanatory analysis, not an operational predictive ranking. Limits: small data (105 plots, 3 years), and the predictive signal may not generalize (a year or region out of sample).
- **Track 3 fit: 9/10.** It is literally the human/feed frontier: the 3 % threshold decides whether the grain ends up on a plate or in a feeder, which directly explains the fear of losing income. Furthermore, lentil is the crop in deficit (43 kt imported) and the 2025 quality was "dégradée par les bioagresseurs" [degraded by pests].
- **Impact fit (30 %): 8/10.** It keeps volume in the human channel, allows collective control at basin scale (which is exactly what Terres Inovia asks for), reduces useless insecticide use (Terres Inovia: treating when temperatures are high is "généralement inutile" [generally useless]) and gives the producer an economic argument. We must avoid promising "pesticide prescription" or "certification".
- **Main risk:** an honest held-out evaluation may give little predictive power. If it passes, it is pitched as "ranking + uncertainty + which data is missing (FabaOdor traps)".

### Idea 5: multispectral drone yield in pea/chickpea trials (US)

- **Innovation: 3/10.** Zhang et al. 2021 already did it with the same trials (R² 0,91/0,80). In the best case we replicate.
- **Track 3 fit: 4/10.** They are breeding trials in the US, not French producers or income. The only credible bridge is "accelerating pulse varietal selection" (10 M€ call, July 2026), but the user would be a breeder, not a producer, and the data are not French.
- **Impact fit: 4/10.** It is indirect and long term.
- It also carries the highest execution risk (radiometric calibration, joining images to plots).

### Idea 4: early departmental forecast (climate + Agreste)

- **Innovation: 5/10.** Agreste (national, monthly) and MARS (country level, includes peas/field beans) already exist. The added value would be departmental, earlier, with intervals and for lentil/chickpea, but yield ML with climate is a standard method.
- **Track 3 fit: 6/10.** It helps plan collection, contracts and outlets for a cooperative, but aggregated data do not distinguish human destination from feed, and yield is not income.
- **Impact fit: 5/10.** It helps resilience and planning, but the link to "more protein for humans" is indirect. Rigor risk: few observations (years × departments), and departmental series for lentil and chickpea may not exist.

### Idea 2: climate analog seasons

- **Innovation: 5/10.** The method is known, but I have not seen it applied to French pulses at department level (Climadiag projects into the future; it does not search for past analogs).
- **Track 3 fit: 6/10.** It is very explainable for the producer ("your season resembles 2016 and 2021, which were bad for lentil"), and that responds to the fear of variability.
- **Impact fit: 5/10.** Risk on the essential AI criterion (20 %): a nearest-neighbour search on climate indices may look like a table lookup. It works better as an explanatory layer for idea 4 than as a standalone project.
- **2 + 4 combined:** innovation 6, fit 6-7.

### Recommendation

**Idea 3** is the one that best fits the track's narrative (the human/feed threshold = income), with the 30 % impact weight and the market gap (there is no bruchid DSS, and in the 2025 edition there was no agricultural project). Nameable clients: Trescartes/Pissier, Berry Graines, Axereal, Terrena (winners of the 2025 call for projects). If the bruchid model shows no signal in the first hours, the 2 + 4 combination is the safest plan B. Idea 5 should be discarded, unless the team wants a pivot to "phenotyping for breeders", which does not fit the track well.
