# French soybean market: is a food-grade intake grader useful? (desk research, 31/10/2026)

Scope: soybean (*Glycine max*) grown in metropolitan France, with a focus on the human-food channel (tofu, soy drinks, desserts). Feed is described only as context.

**Tags.** [V] read on the primary source (page or PDF opened; for Terres Inovia PDFs, opened in a real browser because they return 403 to bots). [F] secondary source, press summary, or a figure I saw only in a search snippet. [NV] my own calculation or inference (inputs shown). Every number carries its season/year and scope.

---

## Key takeaways

1. **Food soy is small and flat: 45 kt/yr, about 11 % of a ~400 kt crop.** FranceAgriMer gives 402 kt production and 45 kt human food (averages 2020/21-2024/25) [S1, V]. The Terres Univia balance shows human food at exactly 45 kt in each of 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24 and 2024/25 [S3, V], which suggests it is a fixed estimate rather than a measured, growing flow [NV].
2. **The crop is shrinking and volatile.** Area peaked at 187 kha in 2020 and was 150 kha in 2025 (-20 %) [S4, V; NV calc]. Agreste forecasts only **298 kt for 2026** (-24.8 % vs 2025) after summer drought [S8, F via Réussir quoting Agreste].
3. **Quality, not demand, hit the food channel in 2025.** 37 % of lots had >1 % green (immature) seeds in 2025 vs 11 % in 2024, "pénalisant pour le débouché en alimentation humaine"; mean protein fell to 39.8 % DM, the lowest in at least 10 years, below the usual >40 % DM food-contract floor [S2, V; S6, V].
4. **The food premium is small.** The interprofessional producer-price observatory finds the food vs feed price gap for soy "très faible" [S11, V]: 343 vs 326 €/t (2019/20) and 617 vs 596 €/t (2022/23) in conventional, gap under 25 €/t [S12, F]. A 2024 Terres Univia/Inovia article quotes ~70 €/t in 2021 among collectors serving both outlets [S5, V].
5. **Food soy is almost all French, under contract, with a few buyers.** There are six soyfood plants (two in the South-West) supplied with French contract soy [S5, V]; the food market is "quasi-exclusivement" French-origin [S7, V]. Olga (Sojasun, Sojade) buys 8-9 kt/yr of 100 % French soy [S15, V]; Alpro Issenheim sources 80 % of its soy from ~400 Alsace farmers [S14, V].
6. **Imports are a feed story.** France imports on average 544 kt of beans and 2.9 Mt of soymeal per year (1.7 Mt meal from Brazil), averages 2020/21-2024/25 [S1, V]; about 90 % of French soy needs are imported [S5, V].
7. **There is no public grading standard for food soy.** Food contracts add operator-specific limits (or zero tolerance) on green, black, broken and half seeds, plus hilum colour, seed size, variety list, non-GMO and protein >=40 % DM [S6, V]. The national quality observatory measures impurities, moisture, oil, protein, TKW and green seeds, but **not** broken or stained seeds [S2, V].

## 3. Imports

- **Beans:** 544 kt/yr on average (2020/21-2024/25) [S1, V]; 705 kt in 2024/25 [S3, V]; 777 kt in 2024/25 (+95 % vs 2023/24) according to Terres Univia OléoProtéines 2025 [S18, F, Terre-net 29/10/2025]. The two 2024/25 figures differ (estimate date or scope) [NV]. Brazil supplies "près de la moitié" of imported beans [S7, V].
- **Meal:** 2.9 Mt/yr of soymeal imported, including 1.7 Mt from Brazil (2020/21-2024/25 average) [S1, V]. 2022/23: 2.6 Mt, of which 1.7 Mt from Brazil, 0.1 Mt from Argentina and ~0.3 Mt from NL/BE (possibly re-exports) [S7, V].
- **Self-sufficiency:** France uses >4 Mt of bean-equivalent soy per year for ~400 kt produced, so ~90 % of needs are imported [S5, V]. Self-sufficiency is 50 % for beans and 5 % for meal [S7, V, citing Agreste 2021-22].
- **Is food soy French? Yes, for whole beans.** "Le marché de l'alimentation humaine du soja est quasi-exclusivement composé de soja d'origine française" [S7, V]. "Le débouché alimentation humaine est fortement tourné vers la graine française" [S5, V]. Olga: "uniquement du soja 100% Français" [S15, V]. Alpro: "does not buy soy on the world market" [S14, V]. Caveat [NV]: imported soy protein isolates and concentrates used in processed foods fall outside this count; I found no data on them.

The observatory (Terres Inovia + Terres Univia, Cap Protéines+) covers about 60-63 % of collected tonnage; its 3rd edition (published 04/06/2026, harvests 2019-2023) says food outlets "tend to be more profitable" for pulses in general, with no soy figure on the page [S10, V]. The 2nd edition's synthesis states: "Pour le soja et la féverole, l'écart de prix entre les débouchés en alimentation humaine et animale est très faible sur la période considérée" [S11, V].

**Value of one misgraded lot [NV]:** 28 t truck x 20 €/t (observatory gap) = ~560 €; x 70 €/t (2021 premium) = ~1,960 €.

**Size of the whole food premium pool [NV]:** 45 kt x 20-70 €/t = 0.9-3.2 M€/yr for all of France.

**Downgrade or réfaction:** the Syndicat de Paris (SPCIG) soybean addendum n° IX (Incograin contracts, in force 01/07/2018) "prévoit un barème de bonification et réfaction" and sets the Bipea 208-1806 impurity method, but the article gives no figures [S19, V]. The contract scale is not public [not found]. A lot that fails food specs is not destroyed; it can go to crushing or feed at the feed price (inference from the existence of two outlets with a <25 €/t gap) [NV]. I found **no published case or statistic** of food soy lots downgraded because of quality [not found, several searches].

## 5. Food-grade quality criteria

- **Base marketing norm (all outlets):** 14 % moisture, 2 % impurities [S6, V; S2, V].
- **Food contracts may add** (Terres Inovia organic soy guide 2023, p. 21) [S6, V]:
  - protein: "40 % minimum sur la matière sèche en général";
  - "taux maximum ou absence totale de graines vertes, noires, graines cassées, demi-graines (exigences variables selon opérateurs)";
  - absence of GMO (detection threshold 0.01 % to 0.1 % depending on the operator);
  - variety list ("très souvent");
  - seed size;
  - hilum colour, "plus particulièrement aux boissons".
- **Grain work by collectors for food** is stricter than for feed: "absence de graines étrangères (autres céréales et oléoprotéagineux, graines adventices avec alcaloïdes), absence de graines vertes, tachées ou fripées", plus GMO and allergen (gluten) control plans [S5, V].
- **Buyers:** Olga picks varieties for "les qualités industrielles et gustatives" (RGT Sphinxa, Siroca, Starbela, Stocata), is IFS- and IP-certified, and wants traceability "de la semence à la cellule terminale" [S15, V]. No numeric spec is public.
- **Comparison, feed spec:** UCAL (coop) feed soybean spec: broken <4 %, impurities <2 %, moisture <=14 % [S20, V]. An Italian food-grade spec sheet (Savi Italo): no foreign bodies, impurities <2 %, protein 34-39 % [S21, V].
- **No public numeric French food spec** for broken, split, stained or hilum colour [not found]. The USDA and Canadian grading guides define splits and damaged kernels [F] and are the closest public references.
- **Who grades and how:** the collector (coop or négoce) samples at farm delivery and measures moisture and impurities. The interprofessional quality observatory (run by Terres Inovia for Terres Univia since 2014) analyses samples taken "lors de la livraison à l'organisme collecteur", at the Ardon lab: 78 samples in 2025, 72 in 2020 [S2, V; S22, V]. It reports impurities, moisture, oil, protein, TKW and green seeds only.
- **2025 harvest quality (78 samples)** [S2, V]:
  - impurities 1.3 % mean (5-year mean 0.7 %); 86 % of lots within the 2 % norm;
  - moisture 12.6 %; 81 % within 14 %;
  - **37 % of lots with >1 % green seeds** (11 % in 2024);
  - protein 39.8 % DM (5-year mean 42.1 %; min 31.1, max 44.1);
  - TKW 164 g.
- **2020 harvest (72 samples)** [S22, V]: 71 % of samples with a known outlet were for feed, so ~29 % for food [NV: 100 - 71]. Food samples come "majoritairement de parcelles irriguées" and had protein 42.5 % DM and 53 % within moisture norms, vs 81 % for feed samples.

## 6. Actors

- **Interprofession and institute:** Terres Univia (interprofession; runs the quality and price observatories) and Terres Inovia (technical institute, Ardon lab) [S1, V; S2, V]. Producers are represented by the FOP [S1, V].
- **Collectors:** 124 cooperatives and 418 négociants collect oilseeds (all species, 2024/25); 71,000 oilseed deliverers [S1, V]. No soy-only count [not found].
- **Soyfood plants:** six in total, two in the South-West [S5, V]. Identified:
  - Olga (ex-Triballat Noyal; Sojasun, Sojade; near Rennes): 8-9 kt of French soy per year, about 250 direct producers (group-wide) [S15, V; F for the 250 producers, Wikipedia snippet]. Sojasun's filière has existed since 1995 [S23, V].
  - Alpro / Danone (Sojinal, Issenheim, Alsace): ~200 kt of plant drinks per year; ~400 contracted Alsace farmers on ~6,000 ha; 80 % conventional soy from Alsace, 20 % organic from the Toulouse area [S14, V]. My estimate: 6,000 ha x ~2.6 t/ha = ~15.6 kt, i.e. ~19.5 kt if that is 80 % of supply [NV, assumes all Alsace soy goes to Alpro].
  - Nutrition et Soja / Soy brand (Revel, Haute-Garonne) [F]; Sojami (Agen, French soy within 250 km) [F].
  - Olga + Alpro alone ≈ 28 kt, about 60 % of the 45 kt [NV]. **The food channel is a handful of buyers.**
- **Coops with soy filières:** Vivadour and Maïsadour (Graine d'Alliance, 100 % South-West non-GMO soy, mainly for feed) [F]; Euralis (Sojalim, 25 kt/yr including 5 kt organic, feed) [F]; Valfrance [S24, V].
- **Public policy:** the Stratégie nationale pour les protéines végétales (2020) aims to double legume area to 8 % of UAA by 2030 [F, ministry docs]; the minister's 2025 statement says 10 % of UAA by 2030 [S17, V]. **I found no soy-specific target in the national plan** [S25, V: the charter mentions soy only as the import to replace]. The only soy-specific target was the interprofession's 2017 goal: 250 kha by 2025, to replace 500 kt of imported non-GMO meal for feed [S16, V]. It was missed. The 2025 "Projets territoriaux filières légumineuses" call funded 10 projects for 11.7 M€; only Texipro (texturised protein) explicitly cites soy [S17, V].

## 7. How big is the food chain, and what is the bottleneck?

- **Size [NV, from the sources above]:** ~45 kt/yr of beans; six plants; two buyers take ~60 %; a few collectors run the food contracts. At ~2.6 t/ha that is ~17 kha, i.e. ~11 % of the soy area.
- **Evidence for a quality bottleneck:**
  - 2025: 37 % of lots >1 % green seeds, flagged as penalising for food [S2, V].
  - Protein at a 10-year low, 39.8 % DM, under the usual 40 % food floor [S2, V; S6, V].
  - Food soy depends on irrigated fields [S22, V], which are exposed to drought and water restrictions [NV].
  - Food contracts demand near-zero green, black, broken and half seeds [S6, V].
- **Evidence for a demand or supply bottleneck rather than grading:**
  - The food volume is a flat 45 kt in all four balance years [S3, V].
  - The food/feed price gap is under 25 €/t [S11, V; S12, F], so the farmer's incentive to aim for food is weak.
  - Buyers rely on contracts, variety lists and their own sorting rather than on intake grading [S5, V; S15, V].
  - Supply itself is shrinking (2026: 298 kt) [S8, F].
  - Demand is growing but modest: Alpro says consumption tripled 2013-2020 [S14, V]; plant-based alternatives (tofu, patties) in retail were 6,900 t in 2024 (+24 %) [S18, F].
- **Lots lost from the food channel:** no quantified public evidence [not found]. The 2025 green-seed rate is the best proxy. If 37 % of lots exceed 1 % green seeds and food contracts want near-zero, a large share of 2025 lots could not go to food without sorting [NV].
- **My reading [NV]:** quality limits food soy in bad years (heat or drought), but the chain is small, contract-based and concentrated. The binding constraints are volume (area and yield) and the thin premium, not the lack of a fast grading tool.

## Arguments against / limitations

1. **Tiny addressable market.** About 45 kt/yr of food soy and a premium pool of ~1-3 M€/yr for all of France [NV]. A tool that saves one 28 t truck from downgrading is worth ~560-1,960 € [NV].
2. **Thin premium.** The official observatory finds the food vs feed gap "très faible" (<25 €/t) [S11, S12]. Misgrading a lot costs little, so collectors have little reason to pay for better intake grading.
3. **Few buyers who already control quality.** Olga and Alpro take ~60 % of the volume [NV]. They pre-select varieties and use contracts and plant-level sorting [S5, S15]. The grading need sits mostly inside 2-6 plants, not at hundreds of silos.
4. **Wrong limiting criteria.** In 2025 the food-limiting factors were green seeds and protein [S2]. Protein needs NIR, not a photo. Green seeds are visible, which is a plus: the "immature" class maps directly. Hilum colour and seed size are also visible but are mostly set by variety [S6, S15].
5. **Not measured today.** The national observatory does not track broken or stained seeds [S2], so there is no French baseline to show the problem is common. No public spec gives thresholds to grade against.
6. **Shrinking supply.** Area is -20 % since 2020 and 2026 production is forecast at ~298 kt [S4, S8]. The food channel may shrink with it.
7. **Track 3 risk.** Most French soy and nearly all imports go to feed [S1, S3]. A soy-focused pitch can look like a feed project. The food share (~11 %) must be stated explicitly.
8. **Limitations of this research:**
   - I could not open the Agreste 2026 note (the 298 kt comes via Réussir), the observatory full PDFs (on request only), or SPCIG addendum IX (members only).
   - Terres Inovia PDFs return 403 to curl and WebFetch; I read them in Chrome with pdf.js text extraction.
   - The Alpro tonnage is my estimate.
   - The 45 kt food figure looks like a fixed FranceAgriMer estimate.
