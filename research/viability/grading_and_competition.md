# Grading practice, costs and competition: seed defect imaging for soybean and pulses

Research date: 2026-10-31. Scope: French intake (organismes stockeurs, cooperatives) for soybean, pea, faba bean, lentil, using Canada (CGC), Australia (GTA) and the US (FGIS) as process references.

Tags: **[V]** read on the primary source (page or PDF opened). **[F]** secondary source or search snippet, not opened. **[NV]** our own inference.

## Key takeaways

- **There is no free niche for "AI pulse grading" in general. There is still an open slot for France/EU soybean and pulses.** Cropify (Australia) already sells AI grading for lentils and chickpeas at receival. Its customers include GrainCorp, Louis Dreyfus, ETG Wimpak and Shannon Bros, it plans up to 25 Opal units for the 2026 harvest, and the Grain Trade Australia Trading Standards Committee endorsed it in August 2026 [V S1, V S2, F S3]. Faba beans are announced for 2027, and Canada and the US are its next markets [V S2, V S1].
- **In France, Inarix is the incumbent channel and does not yet cover soy or pulses.** Its public catalogue and credit price list cover only wheat, durum, barley and corn [V S4, V S5]. It has worked on soybean variety identification in Canada (Protein Industries Canada, 2025-2026) [V S6], and Meta reports ~600 silos and an Incograin-certified barley variety method [V S7]. Inarix is the most likely French competitor to add soy and pulses.
- **Ground Truth Ag (Canada) has yellow peas, red lentils, soybeans and faba beans "in development",** on a 1-2 kg benchtop unit priced at about CAD 15,000 plus about CAD 1,500 a year [V S8, V S9]. The lab imaging incumbents are cereal-only or generic: Cgrain lists only small grains [V S10], EyeFoss lists wheat, barley and durum [V S11], and SeedCount has a lentil module [V S12].
- **Image results reach "official" status only through heavy QA, and the human handpick stays the final reference.** In the US, FGIS approved Cgrain for one rice factor (April 2026) with acceptance tests, daily check samples, weekly master-instrument monitoring, and a rule that board appeals are "handpicked as is the standard reference method" [V S13]. GTA endorsed Cropify as worth "commercial consideration" [F S3]. That gives it standing as a field method but does not make it the arbitration reference [NV].
- **Bruchid damage is partly invisible to any photo.** LABOSEM data show that dissection finds 11.8 to 13.5 % bruched peas where a visual count finds 3.8 to 4.1 % (faba: 6.4 to 6.8 % vs 3.9 to 4.8 %) [V S14]. A phone photo can at most match the visual count (exit holes, opercula). It cannot replace dissection for infestation [NV].
- **The lab alternative is cheap per lot, not per truck.** The Incograin (Syndicat de Paris) arbitration lab charges 130 € HT for an impurity analysis of pulses (feed or food) and 120 € HT for oilseeds, plus 10 € per sample handled [V S15]. Seasonal silo agents earn about 11.9 to 12.9 € gross an hour [F S16]. The value of a tool is throughput and consistency at the harvest peak, not labour saved [NV].
- **The production bar is far above a classifier.** Inarix limits wheat broken-grain analysis to two phone models [V S5]. It runs "Master sites" that send about 2 calibration acquisitions a day per species, paired with Infratec readings [V S17]. FGIS requires fixed, levelled, dust-free installation, and re-validation after any move [V S13].

## 1. How intake grading works (France, with foreign references)

**France, general intake flow**
- The national qualification for silo agents (CQP Agent de silo, RNCP40233, level 4, decided 2025-02-28) describes harvest intake as "pesée, échantillonnage et prélèvement" followed by "mesure de l'humidité, du taux d'impuretés, du taux de protéines, etc.", with acceptance through "techniques d'agréage" using analysis tools [V S18]. Pulses are not mentioned anywhere in the qualification [V S18].
- Intake is done truck by truck. Inarix's French offer is built around "analyses qualité benne par benne" and an "allotement" plan, with each storage cell visible [F S19 (sponsored article, 2025-05-26)]. Inarix's calibration network photographs the intake Infratec screen (protein, moisture, test weight) for the same sample [V S17]. So whole-grain NIR at the intake desk is the standard instrument for cereals [NV, from S17].
- Sampling reference: NF EN ISO 24333 (cereals sampling, static or dynamic, manual or mechanical; it excludes seeds). A simplified French routine method, NF V03-777 (2013), exists for commercial contracts [F S20]. We found no public coop procedure giving the number of probe points per trailer [NV: not found].
- Contract layer: the Syndicat de Paris (Incograin) publishes contract forms, RUFRA (grains, oilseeds, proteaginous crops, legumes, 2020 edition) and RULEGS (dry pulses, 2021 edition). Its technical addenda include no. 7 "Protéagineux (alimentation du bétail)", no. 7 bis "Protéagineux (alimentation humaine)" and no. 9 "Graines de soja crues". BIPEA selects and monitors the labs, and disputes go to the Chambre Arbitrale Internationale de Paris (CAIP) [V S21]. The addenda texts are members-only, so we could not read the thresholds [V S21].
- In 2021 the Syndicat planned "un ou plusieurs addenda techniques pour les légumes secs avec examen de la méthode ISO 605" [V S22 (2021-08-06)].
- Soybean (Terres Inovia observatory, 2025 harvest): 78 samples were analysed by the Terres Inovia lab at Ardon for impurities, moisture, oil, protein and thousand-kernel weight [F S23]. For food-grade soy contracts, Terres Inovia notes protein is "généralement supérieur à 40 % sur matière sèche", and buyers may ask for hilum colour and seed size. Seed cleanliness (staining by nightshade and Phytolacca berries) is "particulièrement regardée" [F S24]. So for soy at intake, the defect-type criteria are impurities, broken and split seeds, staining and hilum colour, while the price drivers are NIR measures [NV].
- Pulses for food: an older figure says Egypt requires 1 % bruched faba beans while France can usually guarantee only 3 %, and that French faba exports fell from 245 kt (2010/11) to 87 kt (2014/15) mainly because of bruchids [F S25]. The 2025 Terres Inovia faba sheet reports that bruched seeds are "assez présentes" in winter faba lots and nearly absent in spring faba [F S25].

**Who does it and how long it takes**
- Seasonal silo agents, often hired as beginners for 45 days to 2 months [F S16]. Australian receival samplers are described as often "young people" with "fairly quick training" under truck pressure, and grading varies "from person to person" and between sites [V S26 (2025-03-20)].
- Time: a pulse classification "can take up to 20 minutes at receival" (Australia) [F S3]. Cropify claims about 5.3 minutes per sample [V S27 (2026-09-04)]. Inarix claims one minute for wheat broken grains "compared to 5 to 10 minutes for a trained operator" (vendor claim) [V S5]. LABOSEM bruchid counts use 200 grains for lentils and 250 or 500 for proteaginous crops (visual), or 4x50 / 8x50 grains (dissection after soaking), done by staff with "contrôle annuel des compétences" [V S14].

**Intake vs lab**
- Intake: weight, moisture, impurities, protein (and test weight for cereals) [V S18]. Lab or arbitration: the same analyses on retained samples. The Incograin price list covers moisture, impurities (feed and food) and protein for proteaginous crops, and acidity, erucic acid, oil, moisture and impurities for oilseeds [V S15]. Bruchid counts are a specialised lab service (LABOSEM) [V S14].

**Process references abroad**
- Canada: at a licensed primary elevator, "Un sous-échantillon d'au moins 1 kilogramme est requis". The operator grades it, the producer may watch, and the producer can ask for a CGC "détermination définitive" and then appeal [V S28].
- Canada, lentils: grading is done on portions divided from the clean sample with a Boerner divider. Portions are 25-100 g for damage, 50-250 g for foreign material and 50-100 g for contrasting colours. CGC prepares visual standards [V S29]. Tolerances, for example lentils other than red: total damage 2.0 / 3.5 / 10.0 %, stained 1 / 4 % / no limit, heated 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 % for No. 1 / 2 / 3 [V S30]. Suspect seeds are cut to confirm internal damage [F S29].
- US: FGIS keeps interpretive line samples for visual factors on lentils [F S31].
- Australia: GTA publishes pulse receival and export standards, including general procedures for sampling and assessment [V S32 (2026/27 guide)].

[NV] The arbitration-lab cost (about 140 € per analysis) applies per lot and per dispute, not per truck. A typical coop dispute over one truck is more likely settled by re-analysis of the retained sample than by arbitration. Labour per manual count is small. The economic case therefore rests on (a) avoiding truck queues at harvest, (b) consistency between sites, and (c) traceable digital evidence that cuts disputes and supports food-grade segregation. Labour savings are not the case.

## 4. Regulatory and contractual acceptance

- **US (most explicit precedent):** FGIS PN-26-03 (2026-04-27) approves the Cgrain Value only for whole kernels in medium-grain rough rice, and the sorting option is not approved. It requires an acceptance test against "master" values, three sets of daily check samples, weekly market samples to master instruments, and monthly handpick comparisons. "WK appeal inspections will be performed using this instrument, however board appeal inspections will be handpicked as is the standard reference method." [V S13]. The route is the FGIS Inspection Technology Evaluation (submission, evaluation, verification, certificate of conformance) [F S48].
- **Australia:** the GTA Trading Standards Committee endorsed Cropify Opal as worth "commercial consideration" for bulk handlers receiving pulses. It was described as the first such recognition for an AI unit "which can be calibrated for use in sample stands", after an earlier endorsement of ZoomAgri Zoom One for specific barley varieties [F S3]. The endorsement lets participants "consider the system for commercial use" [V S1].
- **France:** Inarix's barley variety method was "officially certified by Incograin (Syndicat de Paris)" [V S7]. Incograin's reference labs are selected by BIPEA, and disputes go to CAIP arbitration [V S21]. [NV] So an image method can become a contractually recognised method in France through Incograin. A defect measurement for pulses or soy would need its own recognition, and the arbitration counter-analysis would still go through a BIPEA-referenced lab using the reference method.
- **Standards:** ISO 605:1991 (pulses: impurities, size, foreign odours, insects, species and variety) is the pulse test-method standard, and a revision is at preliminary stage [F S49]. ISO/TC 34/SC 4 has a working group, **WG 13 "Image analysis"**, whose active project is ISO/CD 11746-2 (rice kernel biometrics by image analysis, started 2025-09-18) [F S50]. We found no ISO, NF or Incograin standard for image-based defect measurement of pulses or soybean [NV: not found].
- **Conclusion [NV]:** in all three jurisdictions an image system can be an accepted field or first-instance method. The final arbitration reference stays the human or handpick method. Plan for "pre-screen and evidence first, recognised method later, after multi-season validation".

## 5. Business models and adoption

- **Inarix:** annual licence per user plus prepaid credit packs. Credits are tied to a licence and lost when it expires, and unit prices fall with volume [V S5, V S51]. Public figure: "over 600 silos across more than 10 countries", "approximately 40 % of the French barley industry" (Meta, 2024-12-06) [V S7]. French distribution partners are EMC2, Ceresia, Bodin Négoce and Calipso, and farmers have had direct access since the 2025 harvest [F S19]. Inarix signed an exclusive Americas partnership with AXON Americas on 2026-02-18 [V S4].
- **Hardware + subscription:** Ground Truth Ag (about CAD 15k + 1.5k/yr) [V S9] and GrainSense (3,900 € + 250 €/yr) [F S33].
- **Per-sample:** Nebulaa (Rs 150-200/sample) [F S34], ZoomAgri (about $15/sample, press) [F S39].
- **Unit deployment at receival sites:** Cropify (units at sample stands, multi-site dashboard; price not disclosed) [V S1].
- **Capital sale:** optical sorters at packers (Bühler, TOMRA) [V S43].

## 6. What a production tool needs beyond a classifier

Each item below comes from what incumbents or regulators already require:

1. **Controlled imaging.** FGIS: fixed install, level surface, 10-30 °C, 20-80 % RH, no moving after install, a set background colour [V S13]. Inarix: approved phone list, and broken-grain analysis only on 2 phone models [V S5]; photo protocol: flat spread, no shiny surfaces, phone held parallel [V S52]. [NV] A phone tool needs a cheap standard box or tray (background, diffuse light, scale and colour reference) and a phone allow-list.
2. **Sample size and statistics.** Reference portions are 25-100 g for lentil damage (CGC) and 200-500 grains for LABOSEM bruchid counts [V S29, V S14]. [NV] A confidence interval on % defects needs a seed count reported per sample (binomial CI). At 2 % defects, 500 seeds give roughly ±1.2 points (95 %), which is not enough to separate a 1 % from a 3 % threshold on one photo. Plan for several photos per sample.
3. **Calibration against the reference method.** Acceptance tests in several ranges, daily check samples, monthly handpick comparisons [V S13]. Inarix "Master sites" send about 2 acquisitions per day per species, paired with reference values [V S17]. Seasonal model drift: "a variety well recognized in previous years is less easily identified at the beginning of the harvest", and models are updated daily [V S53].
4. **Coverage of invisible defects.** Internal bruchid infestation needs dissection [V S14]. Heated or internal damage in lentils is confirmed by cutting [F S29]. [NV] The product must state which defect classes it measures and which it cannot.
5. **Traceability.** Sample ID by barcode or QR, stored images, export [V S13, V S17]. Cropify sells "a digital record of each load" [V S1].
6. **Integration with silo software.** Inarix offers an API, a web portal and an allotment tool [V S4, V S5]. [NV] French coops run intake software (weighbridge plus quality capture), so results must reach the intake ticket, or the tool stays a side app.
7. **Multi-site, multi-season validation.** Inarix master sites are organised by region "to represent the diversity of terroirs" [V S17]. Cropify spread from SA/VIC to NSW/QLD before endorsement [V S1]. [NV] At least 2 harvests and several regions are needed before any recognition.
8. **Seasonality.** Intake is concentrated in harvest windows (seasonal staff hired for 45 days to 2 months [F S16]). [NV] Revenue per site is seasonal: soybean in September-October, peas and faba in July, lentils in July-August. Credit packs fit this pattern better than monthly SaaS.
9. **Connectivity.** Inarix requires an internet connection for every analysis [V S5]. [NV] Rural silos with weak signal need an offline mode or an on-device model.

## Arguments against / limitations

- **The niche is small and seasonal [NV].** French food-grade pulse consumption is about 130 kt a year [F S54]. Soy and pulse areas are small next to cereals, so per-site volumes are low.
- **A strong pulse-specific incumbent exists.** Cropify has commercial references with global traders (LDC) and an industry endorsement, and plans to expand abroad [V S1]. Inarix already sits in French coops and could add soy defects on the same licence and credit model [V S5, V S7].
- **The price-relevant intake measures for soy are NIR (protein, oil, moisture), not defects** [F S23]. A defect tool adds a second device or app at the intake desk unless it also does impurities [NV].
- **Bruchid damage, the key food-grade defect for pea and faba, is underestimated 2-3x by any external visual method** [V S14].
- **Contractual reference stays human or handpick** (FGIS board appeal) [V S13]. Incograin recognition is case by case [V S7]. We could not read Incograin addenda 7 bis and 9 (members only), so the exact food-grade thresholds in French contracts are unverified [V S21].
- **Phone variability is a real engineering cost.** Even Inarix restricts some analyses to two phone models [V S5].
- **Unverified items:** LABOSEM and Eurofins prices; Zeutec, Satake and Videometer specifics; ZoomAgri's soybean product; the full GTA endorsement wording (Grain Central blocked automated access); French per-trailer probing rules; Codex CXS 171 and the GrainSense price were not re-verified here.
