# French pulses for human food: market, quality losses, policy, bottlenecks

Scope: lentil, chickpea, dry beans, pea, faba bean grown in France, for HUMAN food. Purpose: test the
hypothesis "grading French pulses (lentil first) by phone photo would be more valuable than grading soybean".
Research date: 2026-10-31.

Tags: **[V]** read on the primary source (page or PDF opened). **[F]** secondary source, press, or search
snippet. **[NV]** own calculation or inference, inputs shown. **[TEAM]** from the team's own verified notes,
not re-opened here.

---

## Key takeaways

- **Lentil is a small crop next to soy.** 2024: lentil 34,216 ha, chickpea 27,494 ha, soy 153,000 ha
  (398 kt) [V, S1, S3]. Estimated lentil output 40-59 kt [NV], so farm-gate value about 32-51 M€/yr for lentil
  vs about 159 M€/yr for soy [NV, inputs in section 3]. Per hectare the two are similar (lentil about
  1,200-1,500 €/ha, soy about 1,040 €/ha) [NV].
- **Lentil is the one pulse with a large import gap.** 2024/25: 42,910 t imported (21,987 t from Canada,
  3,263 t from Turkey) vs 4,628 t exported, a net deficit of 38,282 t, which has grown from 22,030 t in
  2020/21 [V, S1]. Roughly 45-55 % of the lentils eaten in France are imported [NV]. France is a net
  **exporter** of chickpea (+4,461 t), pea and faba [V, S1].
- **Bruchid damage on lentil is high and rising.** 2025 harvest: mean 24.4 % bruchid-damaged grains
  (40 samples taken at delivery to the collector, range 1.0-75.5 %), 36.8 % in Centre-Ouest, the highest
  since the survey began in 2021 [V, S4]. Earlier years in the same chart were about 10-15 % [V, S4].
- **What a downgrade costs (lentil).** Food lentil 806-873 €/t [TEAM, FranceAgriMer] vs a feed-protein
  proxy of 200-260 €/t (pea/faba feed price) [V, S22]: about 550-620 €/t lost on every tonne pushed out of
  food [NV]. If the 24.4 % bruchid fraction is sorted out and sold as feed, that is about 136 €/t harvested
  and roughly 5-8 M€ nationally in 2025 [NV, upper bound, see section 4].
- **Faba bean shows what bruchids can do to a human-food outlet.** Faba exports to Egypt (human food) fell
  from 245 kt (2010/11) to 87 kt (2014/15), "essentiellement" because of bruchid-damaged grain [F, S14].
  Egypt requires under 1 % bruchid grains for food, under 10 % for feed, and in 2017 60 % of French faba lots
  were above 1 % [V, S13]. Today faba exports go mainly to Norway for fish feed (44.9 kt of 58.5 kt in
  2024/25) and domestic faba for human food is only 11 kt [V, S1].
- **Pea for human food is mostly ingredients, not whole grain.** Domestic pea use "human food and non-food
  ingredients" was 136 kt in 2024/25 vs 111 kt feed [V, S1]. Pea + faba human use 146 kt vs 62 kt compound
  feed [V, S2]. The fractionation buyer (Roquette) grades on colour, broken grains and purity and pays a
  +30 €/t premium [F, S21]. Bruchids are not cited as a criterion.
- **Policy is about area, not quality grading.** The 2020 strategy aims to double protein-crop area to 2 Mha
  (8 % of UAA) by 2030, with about 150 M€ from France Relance [V, S10]. The July 2026 wording is 10 % of UAA,
  about 2.7 Mha [V, S11]. Yet "légumes secs" area fell 23 % between 2020 and 2022 (67,930 to 52,200 ha) [V, S10].
- **The barriers cited in the literature are price competition with imports, yield variability, missing
  sorting/storage tools, and low demand** (1.6 kg/adult/yr vs about 5 kg needed for the PNNS "twice a
  week" guideline) [V, S7, S9, S10, S16]. Quality grading as such is never named as the main barrier.
  Sorting **equipment** is named (S9, S10).

### Which ones are mostly for human food?

- **Lentil, chickpea, dry beans**: almost entirely human food. The 2015-2019 Terres Univia chart shows 100 %
  human food for these three [V, S8]. Dry beans "100 % en alimentation humaine" [V, S9].
- **Pea**: mixed. 2024/25 domestic uses: human food + non-food ingredients 136 kt, feed 111 kt, seed 23 kt,
  exports 131 kt (Belgium 55 kt, China 33 kt) [V, S1]. Terres Univia tracks a price series called "pois jaune
  pour pays tiers (alimentation humaine)", destination mainly China [V, S1]. So the Chinese exports are also
  food grade.
- **Faba**: mostly feed now. 2024/25: human food (milling, food ingredients) 11 kt vs feed 144 kt, exports
  59 kt (Norway 44.9 kt, aquaculture) [V, S1].

## 2. Consumption and import dependence

### Per capita
- **1.6 kg per adult per year** of pulses (Nutrimétrie 2024 survey, published 27 May 2026): canned about
  0.7 kg, prepared dishes 0.2, dry 0.2, soups 0.1. Manual workers 0.4 kg vs managers 2.4 kg. Lentil is the
  most eaten pulse, then chickpea. The PNNS guideline ("twice a week") needs about **5 kg/person/yr**
  according to the same source's footnote [V, S7].
- Older figures: 1.7 kg/person/yr vs 7.2 kg in 1920, "half the European average" [V, S9, c. 2015]; 2.3 kg/yr,
  with "70 % of French people do not eat pulses" [V, S8, Agreste 2020]; only 13 % follow the PNNS guideline
  [F, S16, 2021]. Crédoc 2021: 48 % eat pulses at least once a week [F, S15].
- [NV] The gap to the PNNS level is about 3x (5 / 1.6).

### Market volumes 2024 (Terres Univia OléoProtéines 2025) [F, S6]
- Retail: raw dry pulses about 23,000 t (+5 % vs 2023, lentil +8 %); canned 122,000 t (product weight,
  dominated by beans). Out-of-home: canned about 18,000 t. Raw dry pulse volumes are -6 % vs 2019, but value
  is up.

- Canada share of lentil imports: 70 % in 2023/24, 51 % in 2024/25 [NV: 26,373/37,661; 21,987/42,910]. The
  2024/25 jump of EU-origin imports (14,732 t) may include re-shipped Canadian lentils (unverified [NV]).
- Chickpea 2024/25: imports 10,544 t (Mexico 2,586, Canada 1,956, India 1,050), exports 15,005 t, balance
  +4,461 t. France has been a net exporter every year 2021/22-2024/25 [V, S1].
- Pea 2024/25: imports 44,501 t, exports 131,229 t. Faba: imports 35,253 t, exports 58,539 t [V, S1].
- 2015-2019 averages [V, S8]: dry beans import 41,125 t / export 6,156 t; chickpea import 8,520 / export
  11,000; lentil import 29,440 / export 5,260.

### Self-sufficiency
- **Lentil 2024/25** [NV]: apparent consumption = production (40-59 kt) + 42.9 kt imports - 4.6 kt exports =
  78-97 kt. Imports are about 44-55 % of it, and self-sufficiency is about 51-60 %. 2015-2019 average
  [NV, S8]: 34.6 / (34.6 + 29.4 - 5.3) = 59 %. Caveat: the apparent consumption includes seed, stock changes
  and downgraded lots, and it looks high next to the Nutrimétrie per-capita figure. Treat it as an order of
  magnitude.
- **Dry beans** [NV, S8]: 9.7 / (9.7 + 41.1 - 6.2) = 22 % self-sufficient (2015-2019). Older sources:
  "about 13 % of needs French origin" [V, S9]; "70-80 % imported" [F, S16, 2021].
- **Chickpea**: net exporter on volume, but an older source says France exported its chickpeas while
  domestic needs were imported (no chain, calibre mismatch) [V, S9]. In 2021 the large calibre (10-12 mm) was
  still short [F, S16].
- **Green lentil**: "near self-sufficient" for retail; coloured lentils (coral, blond) and the
  food-service/industry segments depend on imports [F, S16, Anils/FNLS 2021].
- **All pulses**: estimates conflict. "France produces only 20 % of the pulses it consumes" [V, S9, c. 2015]
  vs "70 % of our needs" (FNLS president, 2021) [F, S16]. Lentil imports 2023: 39,514 t, mainly Canada,
  Turkey, China [F, S17]. That matches S1's 39,501 t for 2022/23.

## 3. Prices: food grade vs feed or downgraded

| Item | Price | Year, scope | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lentil producer price | 873 €/t (2024/25), 806 €/t (2025/26) | France, FranceAgriMer | [TEAM] |
| Lentil, conventional | 400-650 €/t | Grand Est price reference grid 2025 | [V, S22] |
| Lentil, organic | 900-1,300 €/t | same | [V, S22] |
| Soy | 380-480 €/t (project price 400) | same | [V, S22] |
| Pea / faba | 200-260 €/t (pea project price 230) | same | [V, S22] |
| Pea feed vs food | 250 vs 280 €/t | Terres Inovia 2025 guide | [TEAM] |
| Yellow pea, Roquette contract | about 250 €/t incl. +30 €/t premium for meeting the spec | Hauts-de-France, 2020/21 | [F, S21] |
| Lentil, sorted, organic, at processor | 1,200-1,400 €/t | Normandy conversion guide, 2021 prices | [F, search snippet] |

- No French quote was found for "feed lentil" or "downgraded lentil" (gap). The best proxy is the feed
  protein-crop price, 200-260 €/t.
- **Value lost per tonne of lentil downgraded to feed** [NV]: 806 - 250 = 556 €/t (2025/26), or
  873 - 250 = 623 €/t (2024/25). For a 25 t lot that is about 14-16 k€.
- **Gross product per ha** [NV]: lentil 1.5-1.71 t/ha x 806-873 €/t = 1,209-1,493 €/ha. Soy 2.61 t/ha (2024
  Agreste) x 400 €/t = 1,044 €/ha.
- **National farm-gate value** [NV]: lentil 40-59 kt x 806-873 €/t = 32-51 M€. Soy 398 kt x 400 €/t =
  159 M€. Replacing all 2024/25 lentil imports (42.9 kt) at 806 €/t would be worth about 35 M€.
- **Price volatility**: in 2021 Canadian/US output fell 50 % and lentil prices were expected to double
  [F, S15]. Volatility makes 2-3 year planning hard [F, S16].

### Réfaction or grading grids
- **No public French réfaction grid** for bruchid-damaged or broken lentils was found. Contracts are private,
  set by collectors and packers. Almost all lentil harvest is contracted before sowing [F, S17]. Calibre is
  "generally fixed in the cahiers des charges" [V, S4].
- **Thresholds that do exist**:
  - Faba: under 1 % bruchid grains for human food (Egypt norm), under 10 % for feed [V, S13]. An INRAE
    document cites a 3 % maximum for faba sold for human food [F, search snippet, hal.inrae.fr].
  - Pea/faba, older Terres Inovia/Arvalis guidance: 1-3 % for human food, 10 % for feed [F, Perspectives
    Agricoles 2003, snippet].
  - Lentil field-pressure classes: under 10 % low, 10-20 % medium, over 20 % high (indicative) [V, S19].
  - AOP Lentille verte du Puy: calibre 3.25-5.75 mm, wrinkled and sprouted grains excluded. No numeric
    bruchid tolerance was found [F, search snippet, INAO].
- **Benchmark: the Canadian Grain Commission lentil grades** [V, S20]: total damage max 2.0 % (No. 1), 3.5 %
  (No. 2), 5.0 % (Extra No. 3), 10.0 % (No. 3). Peeled/split/broken has the same limits. Insect parts are
  capped at 0.02 % in every grade. Beyond No. 3 a lot becomes "Sample". Insect-damaged kernels count in
  "damage" per the CGC grading factors [F, snippet]. [NV] By that standard, a French 2025 lot with 24 % damaged
  grains would be "Sample" grade before sorting.

## 4. Economic losses from bruchids

- **Lentil (Bruchus signaticornis)**:
  - 2025: mean 24.4 % damaged grains, 36.8 % Centre-Ouest, 18.6 % Nord-Est, 22.3 % Sud-Ouest, 15.4 % Sud-Est.
    Samples were taken at delivery to the collector, and bruchid damage was measured on 40 of 60 samples. Some
    lots were above 30 % or even 50 % [V, S4].
  - Average yield loss of about 20 %, varying by year and region [V, S18, IntercropVALUES 2024]. An older
    survey found a mean of 11.9 % damaged lentils, with 8 cases in 10 below 20 % and some above 70 % [F,
    snippet]. Parasitism rates were over 20 % in only 1 case in 5 [V, S19, Madrid 2015].
  - Damaged grains cause "déclassement pour les débouchés" [V, S19]. No national tonnage or € figure for lots
    downgraded or exported to lower-value markets was found (gap).
  - **Order of magnitude** [NV]: 2025 output about 51 kt (34,216 ha, 2024 area, x 1.5 t/ha). Removing 24.4 %
    gives about 12.5 kt. At 556 €/t food-to-feed spread, that is about 6.9 M€, so 5-8 M€ across the
    production range. This is an upper bound: 24.4 % is a grain count, holed grains weigh less, and part of the
    damage may be tolerated or blended.
- **Faba (Bruchus rufimanus)**: the human-food export market to Egypt fell 245 kt to 87 kt between 2010/11
  and 2014/15, mainly because of bruchids [F, S14]. 60 % of lots were above 1 % in 2017 [V, S13]. [NV] A
  158 kt drop in a food market (now replaced by feed exports to Norway) is the largest documented
  bruchid-driven loss in French pulses.
- **Pea (Bruchus pisorum)**: affects mainly the human-food and seed outlets. Higher thresholds are tolerated
  for feed, and the effect on yield is small (DRAAF Centre-Val de Loire BSV) [F, snippet].
- **Control is failing in the field**: several insecticides have been withdrawn or lost the "Abeille" label,
  "la lutte chimique préventive est actuellement dans une impasse" [V, S13]. In storage, the options are
  K-Obiol (residues refused by some buyers), phosphine, heat at 50-70 °C, freezing (-20 °C for 15-20 days) or
  CO2. Before shipment, "le nettoyage mécanique, voire le triage optique s'il est disponible, permettent de
  ramener aux normes le taux de grains bruchés" [V, S13].
- **Hidden infestation**: the adult leaves the seed "soit au moment de la récolte, soit au cours du stockage"
  through a round hole [V, S13]. [NV] So at reception some damaged grains still look intact: the larva is
  inside and there is no hole yet. A surface photo will undercount, and the visible rate grows during storage.

## 5. Policy targets and funding

- **Stratégie nationale protéines végétales (Dec 2020)** [V, S10]:
  - Double protein-crop area from 1 Mha (2020) to 2 Mha by 2030, 8 % of UAA.
  - Intermediate target +40 % (+400,000 ha) by 2023.
  - About 150 M€ committed under France Relance: 74 M€ farm equipment aid, 50 M€ chain structuring, 20 M€
    R&D, 3 M€ breeding, 2 M€ Bpifrance, 1.2 M€ (of up to 3 M€) pulse promotion campaign.
- **Evaluation (France Stratégie, Jan 2024)** [V, S10]: protein-crop area was slightly down in 2022 vs 2020
  (1,083,763 to 1,045,800 ha). "Légumes secs" fell from 67,930 to 52,200 ha (-23 %), production from 84 to
  60.5 kt. The committee could not say whether the 2023 target was met.
- **July 2026**: FranceAgriMer opened a 10 M€ call for legume breeding projects (planification écologique)
  [V, S11]. It states: more than 1 Mha currently; "l'ambition est de porter cet assolement à 10 % de la
  surface agricole utile française à l'horizon 2030, soit près de 2,7 millions d'hectares" [V, S11]. One
  stated priority is to "développer une offre de produits locaux en matière de légumes secs (lentilles, pois
  chiche, haricots, fèves...)" [V, S11]. Funding also comes from France 2030, CASDAR and PSN 2023-2027 [V, S11].
- **"2023 version"**: no distinct 2023 plan document was found. The ministry strategy page returned 403. A
  2024 call "Projets territoriaux filières légumineuses" (launched 28 June 2024) is cited [F, snippet]. Verify
  before quoting.
- **PNNS**: HCSP 2017 guidelines, "Légumineuses (lentilles, pois chiches, haricots...): au moins 2 fois par
  semaine", preferring production methods that reduce pesticide exposure [V, S12]. Since 2017 pulses are
  classed as protein foods, not cereals [F, snippet].
- **EGAlim / Climat et Résilience** [F, S24]: public catering must have 50 % "durable et de qualité" products,
  including 20 % organic (private catering from 2024). The weekly vegetarian menu in school catering (trial
  Nov 2019-Oct 2021) is now mandatory. Collective catering is the main outlet for canned pulses, 82 % of
  purchases [F, Terres Univia snippet]. Not checked on Légifrance.
- **Aide couplée** for protein crops and pulses exists under the CAP Strategic Plan [F, chamber fact sheets,
  not opened].

## 6. Pea for human food: whole/split vs protein extraction

- Whole and split pea ("pois de casserie") is not in Terres Univia's pea statistics ("hors pois destinés à
  la casserie") [V, S1]. Older sources describe the domestic split-pea market as self-sufficient [V, S9]. In
  2013 it was a few tens of kt [V, S8 chart, INRAE 2014 seminar]. No recent tonnage found.
- Most human-food pea goes to **ingredients** (fractionation into protein, starch, fibre): "alim. humaine et
  ingrédients non alimentaires" 136 kt in 2024/25 [V, S1]. Roquette (Vic-sur-Aisne) built its chain from 2017
  and aims for 100 % French origin. Natup collected 2,500 t of yellow pea from 780 ha in 2020; Roquette had
  more than 2,000 producers [F, S21]. Stated site capacity is about 250 kt/yr of pea, but sources conflict
  on whether that is the French site alone [F, snippets].
- Belgium is the top pea export destination: 45 % of pea exports, 91 kt/yr average since 2020/21 [V, S2].
  [NV] Cosucra (Warcoing, Belgium) is a pea fractionator, but customs data do not split food from feed, so the
  share going to extraction is unknown.
- **Do visual defects matter for extraction?** Roquette ranks "la couleur des grains, le pourcentage de
  grains cassés et la pureté des lots" first. It excludes some pesticides and requires no soy in the
  rotation (GMO cross-contamination) [F, S21]. Bruchids are not mentioned. [NV] For wet fractionation the
  grain is milled, so bruchid holes are mostly cosmetic. Purity, foreign seeds (soy and allergens), colour and
  insect fragments matter more. A photo grader for this outlet would be checking purity and contamination, not
  holes. The price spread food vs feed is only about 30 €/t (250 vs 280 €/t [TEAM]; +30 €/t premium [F, S21]),
  against about 550 €/t for lentil.

## 7. What actually blocks more French pulses on French plates

Sources consistently list several barriers. Quality grading is not one of them, though sorting capacity is.

1. **Price competition with imports**: imports from Canada, China, India, Australia and Argentina head the
   "Commercialisation" barriers [V, S9]. Lack of economic competitiveness vs world markets, small volumes,
   irregular supply, no futures market [V, S23]. French plots are much smaller than in the US, Canada or
   Argentina [F, S16]. Terres Univia's president cited "la problématique de compétitivité" with imports in
   2021 [F, snippet].
2. **Agronomic risk and yield variability**: "variabilité interannuelle des rendements... frein majeur selon
   les agriculteurs" [V, S9]. 2021 lentil yields fell to one fifth [F, S15]. Few approved pesticides for minor
   crops create technical dead ends (anthracnose on chickpea [V, S9]; bruchids [V, S13]). Breeding is slow and
   variety choice small [V, S23]. The 2026 breeding call aims to "lever le verrou génétique" [V, S11].
3. **Missing storage, sorting and packing**: "Manque d'outils de triage et conditionnement des légumineuses
   pour un débouché en alimentation humaine, accessibles aux agriculteurs", and "nécessité de disposer
   d'outils performants de triage" with "peu de personnels qualifiés" [V, S9]. France Relance funded 50 M€
   because "l'absence de silos pour stocker les récoltes ou d'outils de transformation, manque de débouchés
   rémunérateurs" is a brake [V, S10]. About 50 k€ storage investment per collection site, slower equipment,
   pre-cleaning [F, S16].
4. **Demand**: 1.6 kg/adult/yr, strong social gradient [V, S7]. Image "vieillotte", cooking time, digestibility,
   lack of convenience [V, S9, S23]. Raw dry pulse volume down 6 % vs 2019 [F, S6].
5. **Crop competition**: pulses risk being "phagocyté par le soja", which is more attractive on yields and
   costs [F, S16, FNLS 2021]. No production aid [F, S16].
6. **Coordination and statistics**: poor supply-demand coordination [V, S23]. No SAA code for chickpea,
   poor statistics [V, S9]. Still true: no official national lentil tonnage found for 2024-2025.
7. IDDRI judges the plan protéines insufficient without an economic vision for the sector [F, Terre-net
   snippet]. Not opened.

Not found: a Cour des comptes report on pulses, or a Shift Project or FAO France-specific report on
pulse bottlenecks (searched, nothing relevant).

## Arguments against / limitations

- **The market is small.** Lentil area is about 1/4.5 of soy (34 kha vs 153 kha), farm-gate value about
  32-51 M€ vs about 159 M€ [NV]. The whole lentil import gap is about 35 M€ at producer price [NV]. Chickpea,
  pea and faba are already net-exported, so there is no "import substitution" story for them.
- **The documented brakes are price, yield risk, demand and missing sorting tools.** Grading is not on the
  list. A phone grader does not remove a bruchid, cut costs vs Canadian lentils, or raise consumption.
- **Packers already sort.** Mechanical and optical sorting are the standard fix that "ramène aux normes" the
  bruchid rate [V, S13]. A lot is usually sorted, not rejected. The value of a grader is then in pricing the
  sorting loss at reception (about 136 €/t at 24 % damage [NV]), not in deciding food vs feed.
- **The defect is partly invisible.** Larvae inside grains without exit holes at harvest [V, S13] mean a
  photo undercounts at delivery, which is the point where grading happens. Density sorting and X-ray detect
  hidden infestation. A camera does not.
- **Lentil contracts are signed before sowing** [F, S17] with few collectors, so the "trust between
  thousands of farmers and buyers" problem may be smaller than for commodity crops.
- **Bruchid damage is regional and variable**: 10-15 % in 2021-2024, 24 % in 2025 [V, S4]. A business case
  built on 2025 overstates a typical year.
- **For pea (the largest human-food volume) the food/feed spread is about 30 €/t**, and the extraction
  buyer cares about purity and colour more than holes. Low value per tonne for a grader.
- **Data gaps**: no official lentil or chickpea tonnage for 2024-2025, no French downgraded-lentil price,
  no réfaction grid, no national figure for downgraded lots. Several Terres Inovia pages returned 403 (see
  sources). The lentil self-sufficiency range (51-60 %) depends on an assumed yield.
- **In favour of the hypothesis (for balance):** value per tonne at stake is about 2x soy (806-873 vs about
  400 €/t), and the food-to-feed spread is about 550 €/t. Bruchid damage is at a record and chemical control
  is "dans une impasse" [V, S13]. Lentil is the one pulse with a growing import gap (-22 kt to -38 kt in
  4 years) and a stated policy priority (local légumes secs) [V, S1, S11]. Faba shows that failing bruchid
  thresholds can wipe out a human-food outlet [F, S14; V, S13].
