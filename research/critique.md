# SoyMate: adversarial review (Saturday 31/10/2026, 12:00)

Reviewer stance: skeptical jury member, cooperative intake manager, ML reviewer. Everything below was checked against the repo files. One web check (FAO pulse definition) failed with a 404, so that point rests on the team's own sources. Line numbers refer to `pdftotext -layout docs/soymate_viability_brief.pdf` output ("brief L..").

## Verdict

As it stands, it is **not a top-3 project**. Rigour comes out roughly mid-table (the dataset audit is well above hackathon level). The 30 % impact criterion is weak, and the team's own brief undermines it: grading is not the barrier, and the value pool is a fraction of a M€. The business crop (lentil) is never shown on screen. The PoC crop (soy) is, by the brief's own words, "a weak business", and its food channel is already French and under contract, so it adds nothing to sovereignty. The ML plan merges two datasets whose classes do not overlap, so the 5-class model can learn which dataset a seed came from instead of the defect. The lot-level numbers would come from simulation. The headline economics (1 point of correction pays back in 9-10 trucks) does not hold up against the binomial noise at real lentil damage levels, or against the fact that grading corrections are mostly transfers between farmer and collector. All of this can be fixed partly before Sunday 9:30. The rigour story is the only thing that would make this place today.

## Findings ranked by severity

### Critical

**C1. The 5-class model will learn which dataset a seed came from, not the defect.**
- Problem: "insect hole" exists only in the Indian set (d97rjyk2tz). "Immature" and "stained/spotted" exist only in the Chinese Lin set (v6vzvfszj6). "Sound" comes from both. The two sets differ in capture: macro single-seed 640 px phone photos (Indian), 227x227 crops on a fixed background (Lin). A frozen DINOv2 plus logistic regression separates capture setups almost perfectly, so "hole vs immature" is decided by dataset style.
- Evidence: Indian classes Egg/Hole/Testa/Normal_Damage/Healthy (`research/soybean_bruchid/findings.md` L55-59). Lin classes intact/spotted/immature/broken/skin-damaged (`CONTEXT.md` §6.2 L205). Inside the Indian set the class is also confounded with capture session: 9-11/08 are bruchid only, sound seeds were shot on 13-18/08 with automatic AWB (findings.md L30, L85). Cropping removes the printed date. It does not remove session lighting or seed lot.
- Cost: AI 20 % and rigour 20 %. A CV juror asks "how do you know it isn't detecting the dataset?" and there is no answer.
- Cheapest fix: (1) Train a dataset-ID probe on "sound" seeds only and report its accuracy (expect close to 100 %). Present that as a limit you measured. (2) Report per-class metrics only inside the source dataset, plus a cross-dataset sound-vs-sound false-positive rate. (3) In the Indian set, report hole-vs-sound on the light-background, same-day-range subset (498 light-background bruchid images, findings.md L86). (4) Say plainly that the 5-class model on a third-party photo is uncalibrated.

**C2. No per-seed defect ground truth on any real multi-seed photo, so the demo output is unverified.**
- Problem: R4 measures seed count on real photos and lot-level error on simulated lots. In the demo, every coloured seed on a Ghana or Indonesian photo is a prediction with no ground truth. Simulated lots built by sampling labelled test crops only replay the R2 confusion matrix through a binomial. Simulation removes what actually breaks in the field: resolution shift (Indian seeds at ~50-60 px/mm vs ~10-20 px/mm in tray photos, findings.md L153), shadows, touching seeds, segmentation errors, and per-photo correlated errors. It proves almost nothing beyond R2.
- Evidence: plan R4. hwdtghttp7 does have real per-seed labels (masks intact/crushed/impurity on multi-seed images, `CONTEXT.md` L207, `new_candidates.md` L12). Treating it as "no defect labels" undersells it, though the masks may be model output (new_candidates.md L12).
- Cost: rigour, AI ("what is its margin of error?" is an explicit best practice in the guide, `event/participant_guide.txt` L380-381), and execution.
- Cheapest fix: (a) Use the hwdtghttp7 masks (split by 4-digit ID) for a real lot-level "% crushed" error on 8-10 held-out originals. (b) Hand-label 3 real photos (~150 seeds each, ~20-30 min per photo): one Ghana FarmerSaved, one Lin original, one tray of supermarket seeds. That gives a real lot-level error on ~450 seeds. (c) Keep simulation, but label it synthetic on the slide and never put it next to a "%" claim.

**C3. The headline economics is inside the noise and is mostly a transfer, not value.**
- Problem 1, statistics: the 1-point correction (A3) is far below the sampling error of the sample itself. At the 2025 lentil mean of 24.4 % damage, a 200-grain count (LABOSEM's lentil size, CONTEXT L87) has a 95 % half-width of about ±6 points: 1.96·sqrt(0.244·0.756/200). With ~150 seeds (one Ghana-style photo) it is about ±7 points. Brief §9 (L222-224) computes intervals only at 2 %, the most favourable case, and concludes "~1,000 grains" for a 1-point correction. At 24 %, 1,000 grains still gives ±2.7 points. Neither the tool nor the manual grader can resolve 1 point per truck.
- Problem 2, value: if manual grading is noisy but unbiased, the corrections cancel across trucks. The 156-174 € moves between farmer and collector, it is not created. The collector pays the subscription, so on average it gains nothing unless manual grading has a known bias. Nothing in the files shows a bias direction for visual counts against a photo count: both see only visible damage (brief L249-250).
- Problem 3, internal inconsistency: the value of 1 point on all French lentil is 0.22-0.37 M€/yr (brief L135). That is below the revenue ceiling of 0.72 M€/yr (L139), so customers in aggregate would pay 2-3 times the value created. National lentil trucks (1,430-2,110, L134) divided by the 477 collectors used for the ceiling is about 3-4 trucks per site, against a payback of 9-10 trucks. The 477 are protein-crop collectors (pea/faba, CONTEXT L113), not lentil collectors.
- Problem 4, A7 and the réfaction: A7 (grain count = weight %) is flagged as an upper bound, good. But the brief itself says no réfaction grid for bruchid lentil is known (L261), so the pricing mechanism the value model relies on is unconfirmed.
- Cost: economic viability 20 % and rigour. An intake manager will say: "my optical sorter's reject fraction already tells me the loss by weight after the fact" (sorting is the standard fix, brief L113).
- Cheapest fix: reframe the value as variance and dispute reduction, not "€ per point". Show the binomial table at 5, 15 and 25 % damage and a "seeds needed per sample" figure. Drop "9-10 trucks", or present it as conditional on a documented manual bias that you have not measured. Fix the per-site truck arithmetic.

### Major

**M1. Impact (30 %) is close to empty, and the brief says so.** Brief L243-245: "Grading is not what holds French pulses back. True." The plan explicitly disclaims "stopping food going to feed", which is the only lever that reads as sustainability. What remains is "fairness" over a 0.2-0.4 M€ pool, with no environmental or tonnage figure. For soy, the food channel is "almost all French, under contract" (brief L55-56), so there is no sovereignty gain. The criterion says "benefits commensurate with the evidence" (`participant_guide.txt` L462-464): little benefit, almost no evidence. Cheapest fix: one impact slide with a single quantified, sourced chain, e.g. 2025 lentil sorting loss ~5-8 M€ (upper bound, L137) borne by farmers today without a verifiable measure, so a farmer-verifiable grade supports income (Track 3 literally asks for farmer income, guide L351-352). Make the producer-side check (brief L209) the impact story rather than an extension. Do not claim acreage growth.

**M2. "Soy is the PoC, lentil is the business" reads as a dodge, because lentil never appears.** The only bridge (R3) is soy-to-soy, a capture-setup shift within one species. It says nothing about lens-shaped, green or marbled lentil seed coats. A "stained/spotted" head trained on soy will likely fire on naturally speckled Puy lentils. The brief overstates R3: "it is the measured answer to 'how much French data would lentil need?'" (L46), then calls it a hypothesis on the next line (L47) and in A9 (L187). Cheapest fix, today: buy green lentils and dried soybeans before the shops close. Sunday 1/11 is Toussaint (CONTEXT L57), so it has to be Saturday. Shoot 3-5 trays and report two real numbers on the business crop: segmentation count error, and the false-positive rate on packed lentils (packed product is sorted to <1 % unfit, CONTEXT L104). The plan's "we cannot get our own beans" does not hold for sound seeds. Also email Antoine Gardarin (AgroParisTech, same campus, INRAE bruchid faba/lentil data, CONTEXT L380) now. The odds are low on a holiday weekend, but it costs nothing.

**M3. The "insect hole" class targets a pest that is not a French soy problem, from the most shortcut-prone data.** CONTEXT L110: the Indian pulse beetle "does not seem relevant in France". The brief's own "visible in a photo" row for soy names only green seeds (L71). So one class out of five has no market relevance and the worst data provenance. It is also the class most likely to draw the "Insects" exclusion question (see M5). Cheapest fix: present hole detection as a method demo for the lentil route, with its own caveat. Lead the soy story with immature/green seeds (37 % of 2025 lots >1 % green, brief L66-67), and check that Lin's "immature" really means green, which nothing in the files confirms.

**M4. The food-grade pass/fail cannot be decided from one photo at realistic thresholds.** With 150 seeds and zero defects found, the Wilson 95 % upper bound is 3.84/153.84 ≈ 2.5 %. Certifying ≤1 % needs ~380 seeds with zero defects, and more if any are found. On top of that, a classifier with a 2 % false-positive rate on sound seeds reports ~3 % on a perfectly clean lot. The Wilson interval covers sampling only, not classifier bias, so the "% with interval" looks more precise than it is. Brief L89-90 also says there is no public French food spec for broken or stained soy, so the slider default has no source. Cheapest fix: apply a misclassification correction (Rogan-Gladen, using the measured sensitivity and specificity) or at least show the bias. Show "inconclusive, add N seeds" when the interval straddles the threshold. Label the thresholds as illustrative.

**M5. Eligibility questions are still open in the files.** CONTEXT L73 and §13 Q1 planned to confirm with a mentor on Friday that "Insects" means insects as food. Nothing records an answer. The exclusion is absolute ("not eligible, regardless of its quality", guide L368). Soy also triggers the feed exclusion, which names "replacing imported soy" (guide L364-365): ~89 % of French soy is feed (brief L255). The name "SoyMate" makes this worse. Soybean is also an oilseed in the team's own sources (FranceAgriMer "Fiche filière Oléagineux", brief source [2]) while Track 3 is about pulses (guide L345-348). Cheapest fix: get a one-line WhatsApp or email confirmation from the organisers today on both exclusions, and quote it if asked. Say "food-grade soy for tofu" in the first sentence. Rename the class to "pest exit hole".

**M6. Pre-event results must be declared, and the brief's framing hides them.** The leakage audit, the 80-84 % figure and `resplit_by_original.csv` were produced on 30/10 before the event (findings.md L3; CONTEXT L5, L194: "It is a prior resource: declare it or regenerate it at the event"). R1 is a headline result built on that audit. The guide forbids "challenge-specific results" before the start and requires declaration (L236-242). The brief says "Desk research, 31/10/2026, during the event" (L3) and cites the audits as [43] with no pre-event flag. Cheapest fix: regenerate the split at the event with a committed script, and list the audit as a declared prior resource on the submission sheet.

**M7. CONTEXT.md is still labelled "single source of truth" and contradicts the pivot.** It says the product and demo are about French pea and lentil and that soybean appears "only as training data" (CONTEXT L33). It plans own photos and a transfer curve to pea/lentil (§5, §8, §11), a pitch value line of "~840 € per pea truck" (§11), and the Inarix answer "we found nothing in pulses", which the README says to correct (viability/README §7, "not applied yet"). The soy audit's suggested pitch line says "validated it on French grains photographed here" (findings.md L206), which would now be false. CONTEXT also has a "Rule for agents" (L12). Any LLM or teammate drafting the deck from it will produce pre-pivot claims, and the guide treats fabricated results as a disqualification risk (L415-416). Cheapest fix: a 3-line banner at the top of CONTEXT ("pre-pivot, superseded by the brief and the plan for §1, 5, 8-12") and one deck checklist of allowed claims.

**M8. Data claims stated more strongly than the files support.**
- "No French data exists publicly": the team's own audit found Radio_lentille (Terres Inovia, French bruchid-damaged lentil, 807 X-rays plus count spreadsheets, MIT, auto-gated) and called it "gold for the pitch" (`other_datasets/findings.md` L12, L39-45). The download script skips it without HF_TOKEN (`pipeline/download_datasets.py` L184-185, L201-202). Accept the terms and pull it: its counts would give a real distribution of lot-level damage for the simulated lots.
- "Record 24.4 % per lot" (brief L21): it is a mean over 40 samples (CONTEXT L93). The chart covers 2021-2025, so as far as the files show "record" spans at most five harvests.
- "France imports about half of its lentils" (L22) depends on A5, an estimated production with "no official figure" (L54).
- R3 class mapping: Indian "Testa cracking" (noisiest class, findings.md L97) is not Lin "broken" (separated pieces) or "skin-damaged". The plan's shared positive class mixes two definitions, so the R3 drop mixes label shift with domain shift. Indian "Healthy" is not "sound" in 26 % of images (findings.md L141). Lin has 290 duplicate groups (CONTEXT L205), so its own random split leaks.

**M9. Competition: no moat, and the incumbent uses the same model.** Inarix runs on DINOv2 (brief source [29]; CONTEXT L353) with phone capture, 600 silos and a licence model. The team's AI is frozen DINOv2 plus logistic regression. "Not in France yet" is a timing gap, not a moat. Cropify uses bench units at sample stands, not phones (grading_and_competition.md L66). That is a real differentiator (cost, no hardware), and the brief does not use it. The competitor table omits Nebulaa (pulses, India, Rs 150-200 per sample) and ZoomAgri (grading_and_competition.md L69, L78), which a juror can find in a minute. Cheapest fix: position as "phone-only, no hardware, pulse defect module", name the partner or exit route (an Inarix-type channel), and add the two missing rows.

## The 5 jury questions they would currently answer badly

1. "Your hole class comes from one Indian dataset and your immature class from a Chinese one. How do you know the model is not just recognising which dataset a seed came from?" (C1)
2. "On that demo photo, how many of the seeds you coloured red are actually defective? What is your measured error on a real lot photo, not a simulated one?" (C2, M4)
3. "You sell lentil. Have you run your model on a single lentil? And why would soy-to-soy transfer tell us anything about lentil?" (M2)
4. "At 24 % damage, a 200-grain sample is ±6 points. How can a 1-point correction be worth 156 € per truck, and isn't that money just moved from the farmer to the collector?" (C3)
5. "Inarix is already in 600 French silos with phone photos on the same DINOv2 backbone. What stops them shipping this next season, and what is your impact if grading isn't the barrier for French pulses, as your own brief says?" (M9, M1)

## What is genuinely strong

- The leakage and shortcut audit (80-84 % same-original leakage, black-background and printed-date shortcuts, a grouped re-split) is real, checkable rigour that most teams will not have.
- Disciplined provenance: [V]/[F]/[NV]/[A] tags, an assumptions register with "how to check", and anticipated critiques. This matches the guide's "traceable data" and "acknowledged limits" points exactly.
- Honest scoping of physics: "we see the hole, not the larva", backed by the LABOSEM visual vs dissection numbers.
- Sound architecture for CPU-only: segment, crop with mask, frozen features, linear head. Cheap, explainable, and it makes few-shot curves natural.
- Market proof that someone pays (Cropify) and a clearly named user (the intake technician), with the honest admission that France alone does not support a company.
